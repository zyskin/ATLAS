   Introduction
   14073 is from the bottom of the trench at station G
    (see section on 14149). It is a KREEP basalt very like
   14310 (Meyer 1977).  It crystallized 3.88 b.y. ago and
   has been exposed to cosmic rays for 113 m.y.
                                                                        If 14073 (and 14310) are impact melt rocks, then they
   Petrography                                                   might give the best age for the Imbrium impact event.
   Gancarz et al. (1971) describe 14073 as a subophitic,
    intergranular to intersertal, orthopyroxene basalt with                                           Cosmogenic isotopes and exposure ages
  KREEP “affinity”. It is composed of 50% plagioclase,                                                     Turner et al. (1972) calculate an Ar exposure age of
  25% clinopyroxene, 20% orthopyroxene with minor                                                 113 m.y., which requires that the sample be at or near
   ilmenite (2%), troilite, Fe-metal, phosphates(2), Fe-Ti-                                                           the lunar surface for that long or longer.  It was found
   Zr silicates and mesostasis (figure 3 a,b).                                                        buried about 20 cm, and the age of Cone Crater is
                                                   younger (Arvidson et al. 1975).
   14073 may be a clast-free impact melt rock like 14310.
                                           Other Studies
   Chemistry                                                   Morrison et al. (1972) reported a “moderate” number
   Laul et al. (1972) reported the chemical composition                                                          of zap pits on 14073.
    (figure 6), but did not determine the siderophiles. It is
   similar to the composition of 14310 (table 1) and                                                        Burnett et al. (1972) determined the density of solar
   somewhat low in REE compared with KREEP (McKay                                                                 flare tracks.
    et al. 1979).
                                                 Processing
   Radiogenic age dating                                                 Samples 14073 – 14079 were returned in bag 20N,
   Papanastassiou and Wasserburg (1971) determined a                                                        included in ALSRC 1006. There are 6 thin sections
   Rb/Sr isochron (whole rock, plagioclase and                                                              for 14073. Gerry Wasserburg is listed as consortium
   quintessence only) 3.88 ± 0.04 b.y. with I = 0.70034.                                                            chief for 14073.
   Turner et al. (1972) used the Ar/Ar plateau technique
   and determined that plagioclase gave the best result
    (also 3.88 ± 0.05 b.y.).
