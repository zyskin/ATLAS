   Introduction
   Lunar sample 14306 is a clast-rich, crystalline matrix  14306 was photographed on the lunar surface and has
   breccia from Station G, Apollo 14 (about 3 crater  been oriented (Swann et al. 1970).  It was partially
   diameters of Cone Crater). The crystalline matrix   buried. It is somewhat rounded and has micrometeoite
   breccias from Apollo 14 are thought to be from the Fra   crarters on most surfaces. There is a prominent vein
   Mauro Formation, which was recognized as ejecta from   of black glass, probably produced by the last impact.
   the giant Imbrium Basin (Gilbert 1895).             The saw cuts revealed a nice clastic texture (figure 1).

   Radiogenic age dating
   The breccia event represented by 14306 has not been
    dated (probably 3.9 b.y., by analogy to other Apollo
   14 breccias).  Norite clast were dated by ion probe
   (Nemchin et al. 2008).

   Cosmogenic isotopes and exposure ages
   Crozaz et al. (1972) and Drozd et al. (1974) reported a
   cosmic ray exposure age of 25 ± 2 m.y. determined by
    81Kr, supporting the idea that Cone Crater is this age.
